Executive Diagnosis: Why 72.5% of 101 Concept-Instances Repeat

1. Single-Syllabus Explainer Generation (11 of 12 Videos)

Classes 1.1–3.2 and 4.2–6.2 are single-narrator "Explainer" videos synthesized from the same master curriculum outline. Even when prompted to spotlight a specific module topic, the narrator walks through the full arc (Static Paper vs. Runtime → Composer Metaphor → Primitives & Right-to-Left Assignment → Horse & Buggy to Waymos → Two Career Paths) in almost every video.

2. Live Socratic Transcript Grounding (Class 4.1 Spike)

While Classes 4.2 and 6.2 contain 0% novel ideas (every concept appeared earlier), Class 4.1 spikes to 53% novelty (10 brand-new ideas) because it is a 22-minute two-host deep dive grounded in the live October 1 Socratic cohort session (7-line JS shareholder loop, ReferenceError: people is not defined, Leibniz's Calculus Ratiocinator, AI translation layer, and LexClinic API sandbox).

Class 1.1: What is a "Performance" in Law?

Open Class 1.1 ➔
  • I1 Static Paper Illusion vs. Calculus Mindset: Traditional law views contracts as dead paper read left-to-right, hiding the active "calculus of obligations." [50s–140s]
  • I2 Legal Performance Defined: Real-world fulfillment of contractual obligations by human actors (delivering goods, paying funds). [145s–174s]
  • I3 Software Runtime = Legal Performance: Active execution of programmed instructions (runtime) maps 1-to-1 to fulfilling contractual duties (performance). [180s–247s]
  • I4 The Composer Metaphor: Designing verses, choruses, and loops beforehand, hitting "play," and watching the stage performance execute autonomously. [247s–298s]
  • I5 Three Stages of Law (Transportation Metaphor): Direct Law (19th c. horse & buggy) → Mechanized Law (21st c. cars requiring new guardrails/negligence torts) → Automated Law (autonomous self-driving Waymos). [298s–339s]
  • I6 Reducing Societal Transaction Costs: Macroeconomic goal of mechanizing legal performances to eliminate manual verification friction. [339s–411s]

Class 1.2: What is a "Runtime" in Software?

Open Class 1.2 ➔

Repeated from 1.1 (4): I1 Static Paper vs. Calculus I3 Performance = Runtime I4 Composer Metaphor I5 Horse & Buggy → Cars → Waymos

  • I7 Charles Goldfarb & Markup Language: "Grandfather of the internet" digitized legal redline drafting into markup tags (precursor to HTML). [208s–234s]
  • I8 Nick Szabo & Hardcoded Consideration: Hardcoded legal consideration into cryptography to incentivize decentralized network validators. [234s–255s]
  • I9 Logic vs. Grammar Gap ("5 Lessons First"): Lawyers already excel at logic/argumentation; the barrier is purely CS grammar and syntax. [255s–282s]
  • I10 Three Core Data Primitives: Strings (text/names), Numbers/Integers (values/deadlines), and Booleans (true/false gates). [282s–301s]
  • I11 Right-to-Left Variable Assignment & Decoupling Logic from Data: Retraining eyes from left-to-right English to right-to-left code assignment ($500 assigned to rent), separating instructions from data. [302s–337s]
  • I12 Single = (Assignment) vs. Double == (Equality Check): Single = puts a value into a variable container; double == evaluates whether two values are equal. [337s–374s]
  • I13 The And/Or Ambiguity Trap vs. Deterministic if/else Gates: Strategic legal ambiguity (and/or) collapses in strict Boolean if/else logic. [374s–418s]
  • I14 Static Google Sheet vs. Active JavaScript Script: Passive data sitting in a spreadsheet cell vs. dynamic primitives driving an active script. [422s–455s]
  • I15 1980s Unix Philosophy (Text Streams & JSON): Modular programs doing one thing well and communicating via universal plain-text streams. [455s–498s]
  • I16 Two Diverging Career Paths (Engineering vs. Lawyering): Developers acting as specialized legal authorities (like patent agents) vs. traditional lawyers adopting programming syntax. [548s–584s]
